Smart-Pen Data for Formative Assessment

I used classroom records to estimate students’ current homework grades. Smart pens captured handwritten work, and the learning management system (LMS) recorded classroom activity. A separate thesis analysis used the same dataset to predict fluency in the next lesson.

Who and what the records cover

The study followed 186 eighth-graders in four classes at a middle school in Shenzhen. Across 24 weeks, 18 teachers taught Chinese, mathematics, English, physics, biology, history, geography and citizenship education. The resulting dataset contained 7,491 records.

Girls accounted for 46.24% of the sample and boys 53.76%. A Kruskal–Wallis comparison found no statistically significant difference in the selected classes’ exam scores (p > .05).

How a pen trace became a feature

The 20 inputs came from four families: learner and lesson information, past academic performance, LMS activity and measures derived from precision teaching. The latter linked the accuracy of a classroom task to the time taken to answer it.

Fluency = task accuracy ÷ answering time. I also computed each student’s within-class fluency rank and its change from the previous lesson. Together these measures place a paper response in three contexts: pace, standing among classmates and change over time.

What the model comparison revealed

I asked which model could estimate the homework grade, which feature families contributed most, and how the result could be read over time.

From handwriting to a grade

Each training example was labeled with its homework grade: A+, A, B, C or D.

I compared nearest neighbors, support vector machines, random forests, decision trees, multilayer perceptrons and logistic regression. The decision tree recorded 92.862% accuracy, 94.447% recall and 92.857% F1. Achievement and engagement measures figured prominently in the feature analysis.

Interpreting participation and homework together

On day three, interaction reached A while both fluency and the estimated homework grade were D. A teacher looking only at participation would miss that discrepancy. Reviewing the synchronized writing gives the teacher a concrete place to look for an error before choosing feedback.

School partnership

I led the preparation of the partner school’s application materials and helped prepare its presentation for the review panel. The school received RMB 500,000 in technology funding and ranked among the top 40 in Shenzhen’s experimental school selection.
