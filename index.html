Research interests

My background spans computer science and educational technology. My research focuses on AI in education, particularly learner modeling, adaptive learning systems, and human–AI interaction. I use machine learning to study whether students’ interactions with AI can predict what they can do independently. I also design adaptive feedback for interactive learning tools and study how students explain the behavior of the AI systems they build. My work in computer vision includes classroom video analysis and deep learning for image segmentation.

Current research

Ongoing analysis

Predicting Unaided Debugging from Multimodal Evidence

I collected over 6,700 AI dialogue records from 202 participants. I am examining whether students’ tests and code revisions help predict independent debugging performance beyond what prior performance and final-answer correctness can predict.

 Ongoing project

Evaluating Adaptive Feedback in Programming and Database Games

I developed a feedback policy that selects hints or follow-up tasks from learners’ errors and prior attempts. I evaluated its use in game-based activities in two undergraduate computing courses.

 Completed study · Manuscript under consideration

Epistemic Agency and Knowledge Integration in AI Agent Design

In a five-week study with 76 students, I examined how they used retrieved documents, tool outputs, and workflow logs to explain how their AI agents worked.

Jiamin Lou · Sole author

PeerJ Computer Science · In second-round revision

DpSSeg: Deeply supervised multi-scale fusion network for seismic salt segmentation

Difeng Zhu, Jingjing Chen, Jiamin Lou (corresponding author), Yafei Zhang, Bin Zhang, Yuyan Wu

SCIE · 2025 Journal Impact Factor: 2.9 · JCR Q2

I designed and implemented DpSSeg, a model that combines features at multiple scales to identify salt bodies in seismic images. The team evaluated how deep supervision and multi-scale fusion contributed to performance. DpSSeg achieved 79.29% TIoU on the TGS validation set, 4.40 percentage points above the strongest of three retrained baselines, and ran at 106 frames per second on an NVIDIA V100. I wrote and revised the manuscript as corresponding author.
