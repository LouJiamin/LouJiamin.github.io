Next-Class Fluency Prediction

For my master’s thesis, I used 7,491 smart-pen and classroom records from 186 eighth graders to predict students’ fluency in the next lesson. I measured fluency using task accuracy and response time and grouped students by their rank within the class. I built a dashboard so teachers could inspect recent rank changes alongside a forecast.

Building and testing the system

I compared six classifiers for predicting a student’s fluency band in the next lesson. Predictors included prior exam performance, in-class achievement, and current fluency. The random forest achieved 91.99% accuracy and 91.92% weighted F1.

I integrated the model into a Flask and MySQL dashboard. Errors clustered near the boundaries between fluency bands, so I added recent rank histories to help teachers interpret borderline forecasts. I used system logs, case analysis, and interviews with eight teachers to examine how the alerts were used across subjects.

Who and what the records cover

The study followed 186 eighth-graders in four classes at a middle school in Shenzhen. Across 24 weeks, 18 teachers taught Chinese, mathematics, English, physics, biology, history, geography and citizenship education. The resulting dataset contained 7,491 records.

Girls accounted for 46.24% of the sample and boys 53.76%. A Kruskal–Wallis comparison found no statistically significant difference in the selected classes’ exam scores (p > .05).

Prediction errors near rank cutoffs

Misclassifications concentrated near the A/B and B/C cutoffs. A chi-square goodness-of-fit test identified a difference between cases near and away from the cutoffs (p < .01).

I added individual fluency trajectories and a trend indicator alongside the predicted band. In the original system, changes of up to five ranks were stable, six to ten moderately variable, and more than ten highly variable. In the original alert list, three students shared a band C forecast. Their recent histories spanned all three trend categories, giving the teacher a reason to inspect each case individually.

Relationship to the homework assessment study

The formative assessment study used classroom achievement and engagement to estimate the current homework grade. Here the outcome moved forward one lesson, and the important evidence included exam performance as well as classroom achievement. A student’s relative rank could cross a cutoff after a small change. Displaying the underlying history gave the teacher a way to inspect that alert before acting.
